Curriculum Overview


This overview offers a look into how young children learn through play, curiosity, and connection. It connects theory to classroom practice and offers families a resource for supporting social, emotional, intellectual, and physical growth.


Parent Handbook

Philosophy: Washington University Nursery School supports each child's individual development through a play-based, Constructivist curriculum that focuses on the whole child's social, emotional, intellectual, and physical growth. Guided by developmental theorists, the school fosters a nurturing environment where children learn through their own interests and interactions with their environment.

Areas of Development: The program fosters growth in key areas of development. Social and emotional skills are strengthened through collaboration, problem-solving, and self-expression. Intellectual growth is encouraged through language, math, and science activities. Physical development includes both gross and fine motor experiences, while visual arts inspire creativity through paint, clay, collage, and 3D projects.

Enrichment Programs: Learn about our programs in Science, Art, and Music →

How We Teach: Students engage in hands-on, multi-sensory learning experiences that encourage exploration and creativity. Learning centers in art, writing, science, dramatic play, blocks, and literacy provide opportunities for children to develop a range of skills through active participation. The program emphasizes respectful questioning and independent problem-solving, fostering curiosity and critical thinking. Diversity is celebrated through multicultural activities that help students appreciate and understand different cultures and perspectives.

Assessment & Communication: Teachers observe and adapt the curriculum based on students’ individual needs, ensuring that learning experiences are responsive and personalized. Parent-teacher conferences are held twice a year to discuss each child’s progress and development. In addition, ongoing documentation and feedback are shared with families to keep them informed and engaged in their child’s learning journey.
