Parent Handbook Information:



Vision

Washington University Nursery School (WUNS) values children for their curiosity, creativity, and love of play; families for their commitments and aspirations; and teachers for their skill, compassion, and dedication. The school fosters a collaborative community where everyone learns from one another.

Parent–School Partnership

WUNS and families form a supportive team based on honesty, trust, civility, and mutual respect. Open, timely, and constructive communication is essential. By enrolling, families agree to support the school's philosophy and policies and to work with teachers and staff in the best interest of each child. If a constructive partnership cannot be maintained, the school reserves the right to deny continued attendance or re-enrollment.

Staff

WUNS teachers generally hold degrees in early childhood or elementary education and bring extensive experience to the classroom. The school supports ongoing professional development, monthly staff meetings, and CPR/First Aid certification.

Curriculum

WUNS follows a child-centered, play-based curriculum rooted in constructivist theories. Children learn through exploration, sensory experiences, collaborative play, and teacher-guided activities in centers such as art, literacy, science, and dramatic play. Activities are designed to respect each child's pace and background.

Bear Tracks Program

Bear Tracks is an optional half-day extension offering hands-on activities (art, science, movement, literacy) for children ready for longer days. Schedules and enrollment options are available; children must be toilet-trained to participate.

Communication & Assessment

Orientation helps families transition into the school year. Communication occurs through newsletters, the website, emails, and teacher contact. Teachers observe and document children's development; there are two formal parent–teacher conferences each year (fall and spring), and conferences may be scheduled any time as needed.

Discipline

The school emphasizes positive reinforcement, empathy, redirection, and teaching self-regulation. Staff anticipate problematic situations, guide children toward safe choices, and use simple, logical consequences when necessary. Persistent concerns are addressed collaboratively with families.
